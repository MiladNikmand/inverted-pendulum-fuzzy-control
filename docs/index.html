Fuzzy Inverted Pendulum on a Cart

Cart-pendulum  ·  Lagrangian mechanics  ·  4-state nonlinear ODE  ·  Sugeno / Mamdani / Type-2 fuzzy PID  ·  Fuzzy SMC  ·  Adaptive fuzzy  ·  Energy / Backstepping / Sinusoidal / Fuzzy swing-up

1   Physical configuration

A cart of mass mc slides freely along a horizontal track (or around a horizontal ring — the dynamics are identical; the ring prevents the cart hitting hard stops). A pendulum rod of length l and bob mass m hangs from the cart pivot and swings in the vertical plane.

\[ \text{State: }\quad x = [x_c,\;\dot x_c,\;\theta,\;\dot\theta]^\top \qquad \begin{cases} \theta=0 & \text{upright (unstable equilibrium)}\\ \theta=\pi & \text{hanging (stable equilibrium)} \end{cases} \]

\(x_c\) = cart position (m, unbounded)  |  \(F\) = horizontal force on cart (N)  |  Error convention: \(e = +\theta\)

Error convention: \(e = +\theta\) (not \(-\theta\)). The mass-matrix inverse gives \(M^{-1}_{21} < 0\), so positive \(F\) produces negative \(\ddot\theta\). A positive angle error therefore requires positive force: \(F = k_p\theta \Rightarrow \ddot\theta < 0\) (corrective).

2   Equations of motion (Lagrangian)

Applying the Euler-Lagrange equations to the two generalised coordinates (\(x_c\), θ):

\[ (m_c+m)\,\ddot x_c \;+\; ml\,\ddot\theta\cos\theta \;=\; F \;-\; b\,\dot x_c \;-\; ml\,\dot\theta^2\sin\theta \] \[ ml^2\,\ddot\theta \;+\; ml\,\ddot x_c\cos\theta \;=\; mgl\sin\theta \;-\; b_p\,\dot\theta \]

In matrix form: \(M(\theta)\,[\ddot x_c;\,\ddot\theta] = \text{rhs}\)  where  \(M(\theta) = \begin{bmatrix}m_c{+}m & ml\cos\theta\\ ml\cos\theta & ml^2\end{bmatrix}\)

The gravity term \(mgl\sin\theta\) is positive — it drives θ away from 0, confirming the upright is the unstable equilibrium that the controller must maintain.

3   Controllability (rank-4)

At the upright equilibrium (θ = 0, θ̇ = 0) the mass matrix reduces to:

\[ M_{eq} = \begin{bmatrix}m_c+m & ml \\ ml & ml^2\end{bmatrix} \qquad B = M_{eq}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}M_{eq,11}^{-1}\\M_{eq,21}^{-1}\end{bmatrix} \]

The off-diagonal coupling \(ml\) is non-zero at equilibrium. Both entries of \(M_{eq}^{-1}B\) are non-zero, so force \(F\) directly actuates both \(\ddot x_c\) and \(\ddot\theta\) in the linearised model. The controllability matrix has rank 4 — LQR is a fully valid baseline.

For the medium preset (\(m_c=2.5\), \(m=0.2\), \(l=0.5\)): \(M_{eq,21}^{-1} = -0.8\) (rad/s²)/N. A 1 N force produces 0.8 rad/s² of corrective angular acceleration — strong enough to overcome gravity at small angles while remaining numerically stable at dt = 0.005 s.

5   Position regulation and the cascade governor

Balancing the pendulum and parking the cart at a chosen point are two objectives driven by a single force. The system is underactuated: two outputs to place, one input to place them with. They cannot be commanded independently, and every force that corrects \(\theta\) also moves \(x_c\).

Why a direct position term does not work

The obvious fix is to add a correction to the controller output:

\[ F = F_\theta(\theta,\dot\theta) \;+\; k_{p,x}(x_c-x_{ref}) + k_{d,x}\dot x_c \]

This fails, and it fails for a structural reason rather than a tuning one. Near equilibrium the angle loop is very stiff — a tenth of a degree of tilt already commands several newtons — so the position term is either negligible against it or, once large enough to move the cart, large enough to topple the pendulum. There is no separation between the two regimes. Raising \(k_{p,x}\) trades one failure for the other.

The cascade

The working architecture never adds the two objectives together. It puts them on different timescales and lets the faster one serve the slower one:

\[ \underbrace{\theta_{ref} = \mathrm{clamp}\!\left( -k_{p,gov}(x_c-x_{ref}) - k_{d,gov}\dot x_c,\; \pm\theta_{max}\right)}_{\text{outer loop — slow, position}} \] \[ \underbrace{F = F_\theta(\theta-\theta_{ref},\;\dot\theta) }_{\text{inner loop — fast, angle}} \]

The inner loop is unchanged: it still regulates an angle to a reference. The outer loop simply moves that reference. Tilting the pendulum a degree or two puts a horizontal component of gravity on the bob, the cart accelerates in that direction, and the inner loop holds the tilt while it happens. The coupling that makes the problem hard is the same coupling the solution exploits.

At steady state, \(x_c = x_{ref}\) gives \(\theta_{ref}=0\) and the controller reduces exactly to pure balancing. Nothing is left running.

Stability gate

The governor stays switched off until \(|\theta|<5^\circ\) has held continuously for 1.5 s. Before that the pendulum is still recovering from capture and has no margin to spend on moving the cart. If \(\theta\) later leaves that band — after a disturbance, say — the governor disengages and re-arms only once balance is restored. Position regulation is treated as the lower-priority objective throughout, which is what keeps it from ever costing the pendulum.

Where the gains come from

The governor gains are not hand-tuned. The plant is augmented with the integral of position error, \(x_a=[x_c,\;\dot x_c,\;\theta,\;\dot\theta,\;\textstyle\int (x_c-x_{ref})]\), and an LQR solved on that 5-state system. The resulting \(K_5\) already encodes the optimal trade-off between the two objectives, and the cascade gains are read straight off it:

\[ k_{p,gov}=\left|\frac{K_5[1]}{K_5[3]}\right|\cdot\frac{180}{\pi}, \qquad k_{d,gov}=\left|\frac{K_5[2]}{K_5[3]}\right|\cdot\frac{180}{\pi} \]

This is why the analytical design already works before any tuning, and why the optimisation tab reports improvement relative to it rather than relative to an arbitrary starting guess.
